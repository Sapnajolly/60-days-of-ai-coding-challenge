Challenge 01 — Headline Detective

Can you spot a misleading headline?

Before we begin, here's what you need to know:

The Concept

Headlines are the most powerful 10 words in journalism. They're written to stop your scroll — not necessarily to tell the truth. Studies show that 59% of people share articles based on the headline alone, without reading further. Misleading headlines use techniques like omitting context, using charged words, or implying causation where there's only correlation. Knowing this can save you from being misinformed — and from spreading misinformation.

Challenge 02 — Emotion Detector

How does this make you feel?

Before we begin, here's what you need to know:

The Concept

Emotional language bypasses critical thinking. When a post makes you feel outraged, scared, or deeply inspired, your brain prioritises reacting over analyzing. This is called affect heuristic — we judge things as good or bad based on how we feel about them. Media creators know this. They carefully select words, punctuation, even font size to trigger a specific emotional response. Learning to name an emotion while consuming media is the first step to seeing past it.
