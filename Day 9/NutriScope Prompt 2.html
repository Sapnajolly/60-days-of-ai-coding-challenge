Sources & Disclaimer

How NutriScope calculates its numbers, and the limits of what it can tell you.

Educational Disclaimer

NutriScope is an educational self-tracking tool. It does not provide medical, dietetic, or clinical advice, and it is not a substitute for consultation with a registered dietitian, nutritionist, or physician. Calculated targets (BMR, TDEE, macro and micronutrient goals) are population-level estimates based on standard formulas and reference tables — individual requirements vary based on health conditions, genetics, medications, pregnancy/lactation status, and lab results, none of which this tool accounts for. Food composition values are typical averages and will differ from the specific brand, cut, or preparation you actually consume. Do not use NutriScope to diagnose, treat, or manage any medical condition, including diabetes, anemia, kidney disease, or eating disorders. If something here concerns you, please speak with a qualified healthcare professional.

Nutrition Data Sources
  • Indian Council of Medical Research – National Institute of Nutrition (ICMR-NIN): "Dietary Guidelines for Indians" and RDA/EAR tables (used for micronutrient targets)
  • Indian Food Composition Tables (IFCT 2017), National Institute of Nutrition, Hyderabad
  • USDA FoodData Central (fdc.nal.usda.gov) — reference for foods and nutrients not covered by IFCT
  • Mifflin-St Jeor Equation (1990) — basal metabolic rate estimation
  • Institute of Medicine (IOM) Dietary Reference Intakes — cross-reference for vitamin/mineral upper limits

All figures are approximations rounded for display and should be treated as directional, not exact.
