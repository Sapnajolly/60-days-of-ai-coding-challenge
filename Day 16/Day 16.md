## Interpretation Notes (fundamentals-based, not recommendations)

**Valuation**
Nvidia's trailing P/E (~30x) is actually the cheapest of the four on this metric despite being an "AI stock," and its forward P/E (~19.5x) implies the market expects strong earnings growth. Apple trades at a premium multiple for a mature, slow-growth hardware business. Tesla is priced at growth-stock multiples (280x+ trailing) — Expensive by almost any historical or sector benchmark. Intel's P/E is not meaningful because trailing earnings were negative.

**Profitability**
Nvidia and Apple both show exceptional ROE (>100%), though Apple's is inflated by heavy share buybacks reducing its equity base. Tesla's ROE has compressed sharply (4.7%) versus its historical highs. Intel is currently loss-making at the net level (-19.8% net margin), reflecting its foundry/turnaround costs.

**Balance Sheet Health**
All four are within "Safe" D/E territory (<1), with Nvidia essentially debt-free. Apple's current ratio of 1.00 is a watch-point (thin near-term liquidity buffer relative to buybacks/dividends), though this is a long-standing feature of Apple's balance sheet strategy.

**Cash Generation**
Apple and Nvidia are FCF machines; Tesla and Intel generate FCF but at much thinner margins relative to revenue, with Intel's higher capex intensity (foundry buildout) being a genuine constraint.

Moderate — figures pulled from stockanalysis.com/CNBC and cross-checked; some variance exists across sources (e.g., Intel's market cap and P/E show wide dispersion across sources 🚩 verify directly at stockanalysis.com/stocks/intc before acting on it).
